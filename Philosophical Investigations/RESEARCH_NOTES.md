### Checked in the manuscripts on 2026-11-18 [W]

1. MS 107, p. 205: «Die phänomenologische Sprache oder „primäre
   Sprache" wie ich sie nannte schwebt mir jetzt nicht als Ziel vor; ich
   halte sie jetzt nicht mehr für möglich.» (the opening of PR §1). It
   stands in the entry headed «25.», after «2.11.» (p. 180) and day marks
   3 to 24; the notebook has «6.10.29» on p. 153 and «10.1.30» on p. 229.
   So **25 November 1929**: not October (Hintikka & Hintikka), and not
   29 November (Potter's handout).
2. MS 110, p. 214: «Mein Buch soll || kann heißen: Eine Philosophische
   Betrachtung. (Als Haupt-, nicht als Untertitel)», in square brackets,
   in the entry headed «24.» after «22.6.» (p. 195); the notebook has
   «12.1.31» on p. 12. So **24 June 1931**.
3. MS 115, p. 118: «Ende August 36 Philosophische Untersuchungen. Versuch
   einer Umarbeitung.», followed at once by Augustine's description of
   learning language: the 1936 revision already opens as the PI does.
4. MS 115, end of p. 292: «Dieser ganze ‘Versuch einer Umarbeitung’ von

## 1. The texts: composition, editing, publication

### *Philosophische Bemerkungen* (TS 209)

- Manuscripts MSS 105–108 (from 2 Feb 1929) → TS 208, dictated in Vienna
  spring 1930 → TS 209: cuttings of a carbon of TS 208, rearranged and
  pasted into a bound book. The title on TS 209 is Wittgenstein's. [R]
  Pichler 1994; Paul; Keicher.
- Usually taken to be the typescript left with Russell (spring 1930) for
  his report on the grant; Pichler (1994, 2009) and Rothhaupt (2010)
  argue it was TS 208. [R]/[S]
- Given to Moore, then to Rhees (1951). Rhees lost the original in a
  telephone box at Paddington in July 1962; it is still missing. [R]
  Erbacher et al. 2017.
- Edition: ed. Rhees, Blackwell/Suhrkamp 1964 (Kenny 2006 says 1965, alone
  among the sources). English: tr. Hargreaves & White, Blackwell 1975. [R]
- Rhees added the chapters and numbered paragraphs, an analytical table
  of contents, a preface taken from MS 109 (Nov 1930), appendices from
  1933 typescripts (TS 214a, 215a, 215b) and Waismann's notes. "A unified
  product was shaped from a number of disparate sources" (Erbacher). [R]
- Paul: no evidence Wittgenstein meant it as a book; the rearrangement
  hides the abandonment of the "phenomenological language" in 1929. [R]

### *The Big Typescript* (TS 213)

- MSS 105–114 → TS 208, 210, 211 → TS 212 (cuttings under chapter
  headings) → TS 213, retyped from TS 212. 768 pp.; table of contents in
  19 parts and 140 chapters. [R] Bergen; Erbacher.
- Date: "1932–33" (Bergen item record, SEP), "1933" (Bergen table,
  Trinity). Completed summer 1933; Wittgenstein began correcting it by
  hand at once (Schulte). [R]
- The word *Sprachspiel* first in MS 113, March 1932 (Schulte). [R]
- Editions: BNE facsimile and transcription (free since 2016); *Wiener
  Ausgabe* vol. 11, ed. Nedo, Springer 2000 (prints the typescript without
  the handwritten corrections; Kenny); German–English edition by
  Luckhardt & Aue, Blackwell 2005, with the corrections marked. [R]
- The chapter "Philosophie" (§§86–93): German in *Revue Internationale de
  Philosophie* 43 (1989); English ed. Nyman, tr. Luckhardt & Aue,
  *Synthese* 87 (1991) 3–22 (not 1986); reprinted in *Philosophical
  Occasions*, Hackett 1993. [S]/[M] for the last two.

### *Philosophische Grammatik*

- Wittgenstein's revision of TS 213: handwritten corrections on the
  typescript, then the "Umarbeitung" in MS 114ii, continued in MS 115i
  (from 14 Dec 1933 to spring 1934), reordered with MS 140 (date
  disputed: 1933/34 and 1936, 1934 and 1936, or 1934). [R] Schulte;
  Bergen; Trinity.
- A coded note in MS 114: if he died, the material should be published
  as a fragment titled "Philosophische Bemerkungen", dedicated to Francis
  Skinner. [R] Schulte; Paul.
- Edition: ed. Rhees, Blackwell/Suhrkamp 1969 (Kenny: 1970). English: tr.
  Kenny, Blackwell 1974. Title chosen by Rhees from the heading of Band X
  ("Philosophische Grammatik", MSS 113–114). [R]
- Part I reconstructs the revision by following Wittgenstein's
  transposition signs (Keicher: "sehr präzise"; but there is no sign that
  Wittgenstein finished it). The appendix and Part II are Rhees's
  selection by content from 48 of the 140 chapters of TS 213, with other
  typescripts. Rhees left out the chapters "Philosophie",
  "Phänomenologie", "Idealismus etc." [R] Keicher; Paul; Erbacher.

### *The Blue and Brown Books*

- Blue Book (TS 309): dictated to the class in Cambridge, 1933–34, and
  mimeographed. [R] Erbacher; Bergen; [L] Rhees's preface. Paul says it
  was dictated to Ambrose and Skinner (alone among the sources).
- Brown Book (TS 310): dictated to Francis Skinner and Alice Ambrose,
  mid-October 1934 to spring 1935. [R] Bergen; [L] Rhees. Three typed
  copies, shown to close friends and pupils; borrowers made their own
  copies ([L] Rhees). Bergen's record: "a few copies were made without
  Wittgenstein's permission". Skinner's manuscript of the dictation has
  material not in the published text (Trinity Add. MS a.407). [R]
- Rhees's preface ([L], Greek translation): the Blue Book was "a set of
  notes"; the Brown Book Wittgenstein for a time regarded as a draft for
  publication. He sent the Blue Book to Russell with a covering letter.
- German revision (MS 115ii, pp. 118–292), August to autumn 1936, headed
  «Philosophische Untersuchungen. Versuch einer Umarbeitung» ("Ende
  August 36"); it ends «Dieser ganze >Versuch einer Umarbeitung< von
  Seite 118 bis hierher ist nichts wert». [R] Schulte; Trinity; Pichler.
  Wittgenstein to Moore (Nov 1936): the revision was "boring and
  artificial". [R] via Paul.
- *Eine philosophische Betrachtung* is Rhees's title for the German
  edition (Schriften 5, Suhrkamp 1970; year to check), taken from MS 110,
  p. 214, 24 June 1931 (checked above [W]). [R] Keicher. It is
  Wittgenstein's wording for a book in 1931, not the heading of the 1936
  manuscript. That edition mixes Wittgenstein's German (MS 115ii, about
  three quarters) with Petra von Morstein's translation of the rest. [R]
- Published 1958, Blackwell, ed. Rhees, as *Preliminary Studies for the
  "Philosophical Investigations", Generally Known as the Blue and Brown
  Books*. The title is the executors'; the colour names were not
  Wittgenstein's (Rhees). Rhees used Russell's copy with Wittgenstein's
  corrections. [R] Erbacher; Bergen. Rhees: "notes he gave to his pupils,
  and a draft for his own use; that is all" (p. vi). [R] via Erbacher; [L].
- The builders are the Brown Book's first language-game: builder A and
  assistant B, words "cube", "brick", "slab", "column"; game 2 adds
  numerals. [R] LWP text of TS 310; [L]. PI §2 has *Würfel, Säule,
  Platte, Balken* [M]: to check (the vocabulary differs).

### The way into the *Investigations*

- MS 142, the "Urfassung" (§§1–188 approx.), begun Nov 1936 after the
  revision was abandoned; given to his sister Margarete at Christmas 1936;
  rediscovered 1993; now in the Austrian National Library. [R]
- Then TS 220 (Frühfassung, 1937 or 1938), TS 239 (revised early version,
  1937 or 1942–43: sources differ), the Zwischenfassung (1944/45), TS 227
  (Spätfassung, 1945–46). Stage names from Schulte's *Kritisch-genetische
  Edition* (Suhrkamp 2001). [R] Keicher; Erbacher.

## 2. Scholarly readings of the middle period

### Why he left the *Tractatus* picture

- "Some Remarks on Logical Form" (*Proc. Arist. Soc.* Suppl. 9, 1929):
  elementary propositions are no longer independent (degrees, colour
  exclusion, 6.3751). Proops divides the turn into 1928–29 (independence
  given up) and 1931–32 (a "revolutionary break" with the conception of
  analysis: "confusing logical analysis with chemical analysis", Moore's
  notes, Nov 1932). SEP §5: colour exclusion is "credited with being the
  cause", a hedged wording. [R]
- The "phenomenological language" given up: PR §1, «Die
  phänomenologische Sprache oder "primäre Sprache" … schwebt mir jetzt
  nicht als Ziel vor». Hintikka & Hintikka (1986) say October 1929;
  Potter's 2021 handout 29 Nov 1929. The manuscript gives 25 November
  1929 (MS 107, p. 205; checked above [W]).
- Verificationism: discussions with Schlick and Waismann (22 Dec 1929,
  2 Jan 1930), Waismann's "Theses". For Wittgenstein verification was
  constitutive of meaning; for the Circle a demarcation criterion (Uebel,
  SEP). By 1931 he calls his early work "dogmatic" (*WVC* p. 182). [R]
- Influences: Ramsey and Sraffa named in the 1945 preface (read in the
  LWP German). What Sraffa's influence was is disputed (Fann 1969: the
  Neapolitan gesture; Kienzler 1997: sense fixed by use in context; Sen
  2003: anthropological view; Davis 2002, Marion 2005: anecdote-based
  accounts unsatisfying). [R] Unterhuber, ALWS 2007. Brouwer's lecture of
  10 March 1928 "invigorated" him; that it brought him back to philosophy
  is "a gross overstatement" (Rodych, SEP). [R]

### New ideas

- From logic to grammar: rules of use (SEP §4). The calculus conception,
  1929–33 (Rodych); later qualified in PI §81 (we compare language with
  calculi with fixed rules, but language need not be such a calculus). [R]
- Whether the "calculus" picture dominates the early 1930s: Hacker yes;
  Pichler (in Stern ed. 2018) "at best one-sided", the Big Typescript
  already criticises it. [R] via Matar's NDPR review.
- *Übersichtliche Darstellung* named as the method of philosophy in TS 213
  (Philosophy chapter, p. 414), a chapter Rhees left out of the PG. How
  to read it: Hacker (orderly presentation of rules of use) vs. the late
  Baker (2004). [R] Pichler; Martin, *NWR* 5 (2016).
- Meaning as use already in the Blue Book (BB p. 4, "the life of the sign
  … its use"); "craving for generality", "the real source of metaphysics"
  (BB p. 18). [R] SEP §3.3. Family resemblance in the Blue Book: page [M].
- Philosophy as a change of attitude, not an intellectual difficulty
  (TS 213). [R] via Matar.

### Periodisation (the bounds of the "middle period" are disputed, SEP §4)

| Scholar | Proposal |
| :-- | :-- |
| Pitcher 1966 | "Transitional" 1929–35 |
| Hintikka & Hintikka 1986 | one turning point, 1929 |
| Hacker 1986 | 1929–30 as a "bridge"; "disintegration and reconstruction" |
| Stern 1991 | logical atomism → logical holism → practical holism |
| Schulte 1992 | a "middle Wittgenstein" already in the *Tractatus* |
| Kienzler 1997, Nyíri 1997 | the turn in 1931 (summer) |
| Bouwsma 1961 | the Blue/Brown period, 1933–35 |
| von Wright 1979, Schulte 1989 | the new beginning in 1936 |
| Pichler 2004 | a caesura in Nov 1936 (MS 115 → MS 142), of form more than content |
| Pichler 2023 | 1930–35, on style |

Sources: SEP §4; Pichler 2004, pp. 12–14. [R]

- Continuity or break: Baker/Hacker mark a clear break (from logic to
  grammar). The resolute readers (Diamond, Conant; Crary & Read eds.,
  *The New Wittgenstein*, 2000) stress continuity of the therapeutic aim.
  Conant: between "overly zealous mono-Wittgensteinianism and overly
  intractable poly-Wittgensteinianism". Stern ("How Many Wittgensteins?",
  2006): both continuities and discontinuities. IEP: the differences "can
  be exaggerated". [R]
- "Third Wittgenstein": Moyal-Sharrock (ed.), Ashgate 2004, for the work
  after the PI. [R]

### The edited books as evidence

- Hintikka (*Synthese* 87, 1991): Rhees "assembled a medley of materials
  … never intended by Wittgenstein to go together". Kenny: "a little
  unfair"; his own objection is that the edition hides the editing ("Cuts
  are made silently, and transpositions merely hinted at"); he would have
  printed TS 213 as it stood (1976, p. 52). Rhees refused to have Kenny's
  account as an introduction to the English PG. [R] Kenny 2006; Erbacher.
- In Rhees's defence: Erbacher 2019 (*Phil. Inv.* 42(2), 111–145) [S];
  Paul: Part I of the PG is "respectably close" to Wittgenstein's
  intention. [R]
- Pichler 2004 §2.2: are the published books "Wittgensteins oder der
  Herausgeber Werk?" [R]
- The SEP notes that the published editions, not the Nachlass, have been
  the main evidence for the period, and distinguishes notes for lectures,
  notes of lectures, dictations and conversations. [R]

### Blue Book and Brown Book: their place

- Rhees ([L], preface): in the Blue Book language-games sometimes look
  like notations; the Brown Book stresses training before explanation,
  and that "understanding" varies with the games; the games are not
  parts of one general system of language.
- Pichler 2004 §§4.2–4.3: both are didactic "Lehrbücher"; the Brown Book
  and its revision carry out the Big Typescript's method linearly through
  numbered language-games; the break of 1936 was in part a failure of
  form, and the PI partly drops that procedure. Hence the subtitle
  "Preliminary Studies" can mislead. Rhees himself feared readers would
  read the *Blue and Brown Books* instead of the PI (Erbacher). [R]
